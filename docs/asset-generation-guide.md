# Asset generation guide

Everything here is optional until you generate it: the games run silently and the room stills fall back to the rendered ones when a file is missing. Nothing in this guide is downloaded or generated by the build.

## 1. Game audio (AUDIO-04)

Save every file in `client/public/audio/games/` with the exact filename shown. The code looks for these names only.

Tools: Gemini with Google Lyria for the seven music tracks (the same route as `lore-intro.mp3`); jsfxr for the five effects (see Sound effects). After each file, add the generation date and any processing to its row in `CREDITS.md` (rows are already there, marked "pending: Gemini Lyria, generated by Boey"). Lyria output carries Google's SynthID watermark and is used under Google's generative AI terms.

Loudness and format, for every file:

- MP3, stereo, 128 to 192 kbps, 44.1 kHz. Keep each theme under about 1.5 MB.
- Themes: 60 to 90 seconds, about -16 LUFS integrated. They loop in the browser with `loop`, so make the end flow into the start: trim at a bar line, add a 0.3 s crossfade or ask Lyria for "seamless loop" and check the join by playing it twice back to back. In the game they play at half volume, so do not pre-attenuate further.
- Effects: under 1 second (the click under 0.15 s), peak about -3 dBFS, no silence at the start (trim it), no reverb tail, so they feel instant. They play at 60 percent volume.
- Style for all of them: light, 8-bit or lo-fi adjacent, instrumental, no vocals, so they sit beside the hub ambience and the lore intro.

### Sound effects

Shared by all games through `playSfx(name)`.

jsfxr settings for every effect: sample rate **44k** and sample size **16 bit** (8 bit adds hiss). Lower **Gain** until the panel shows **Clipped: 0** (usually about -12 to -16 dB) and use the same gain for all five so they sound equally loud. Leave every slider not named below off or at 0. The values are starting points: match the readout beside each slider, then adjust by ear. Save each effect with **Save As** (.json) or copy its permalink so CREDITS.md can record the exact settings.

Main route: **jsfxr** (sfxr.me), a free browser tool that makes 8-bit effects from presets. Lyria is a music model and returns long clips, so use it only as the fallback below and cut the first note out in an audio editor. For each sound: pick the preset, adjust as noted, press play until it sounds right, then **Export .wav** and convert to MP3 (Audacity: File > Export > MP3, or `ffmpeg -i in.wav -b:a 160k out.mp3`). jsfxr sounds are yours to use; credit them in `CREDITS.md` as "made by Boey with jsfxr (sfxr.me)".

#### Correct call

- Filename: `client/public/audio/games/sfx-correct.mp3`
- Used: a verdict, case or round is right.
- jsfxr (main route), about 0.25 s, two rising notes: **Square**, duty cycle 50%. Envelope: attack 0, sustain 0.06, punch 30%, decay 0.15. Frequency: start about 880 Hz, slide 0. Arpeggiation: frequency mult up about x1.26 (a major third), change speed about 0.06 s.
- Lyria fallback prompt: A short bright 8-bit confirmation chime, two rising notes (major third), clean and friendly, under 0.5 seconds, no reverb tail. Instrumental, 8-bit, matching the hub's light chiptune feel.

#### Wrong call

- Used: a verdict, case or round is wrong.

#### Win

- Filename: `client/public/audio/games/sfx-win.mp3`
- Used: a run, call or climb ends well.
- jsfxr (main route), about 0.8 s, rising and cheerful, step by step:
  1. Click **Powerup**, then **Square**.
  2. Duty cycle: drag the slider fully left so it reads **50.00%**; Sweep stays 0.000%/sec.
  3. Envelope: attack **0.000 sec**, sustain **about 0.35 sec**, punch **about +40%**, decay **about 0.45 sec**.
  4. Frequency: start **about 520 Hz**, slide **about +0.30 8va/sec**, delta slide 0. Min freq. cutoff stays where it is.
  5. Retrigger: drag Rate right from OFF until the waveform box on the right shows **4 separate humps**. Each repeat restarts the rising note, which gives the "da-da-da-daa" climb.
  6. Arpeggiation: Frequency mult stays **OFF** (centre). With retrigger on, an arpeggio would restart on every repeat and sound messy.
  7. Vibrato, flanger, low-pass and high-pass stay **OFF**.
  8. Check **Clipped: 0** (lower Gain if not), then play it. If it sounds too fast, move Retrigger left until you see 3 humps; if too low, raise the start frequency.
- Lyria fallback prompt: A short 8-bit victory jingle, a rising major arpeggio ending on a held bright note, cheerful, about 1 second, no reverb tail. Instrumental, 8-bit, matching the hub's light chiptune feel.

#### Lose

### Checking

1. Run the client (`pnpm dev`), sign in and open a game. Pressing the Sound button in the room's top right should start and stop the theme.
2. Mute, then reload the tab: the theme must stay silent until you press Sound again. Closing the tab forgets the mute.
3. Play a round: the effect should match the call, and nothing should play while muted.
4. If a file is bad or absent, the game stays silent and the Sound button disappears for that room; no console error comes from the app.

## 2. Optional: AI-generated interior stills

Since INT-03 (1 November 2026) every game page shows a still of its room behind the game panel, on every device: the live 3D room is no longer drawn. The current stills (`client/public/interiors/<id>.webp`) were rendered from the project's own Babylon scenes and show an empty board under the panel. A generated image is worth swapping in when it is clearly better and still looks like the same wharf. Botnet Tag has its own 3D arena and needs no still.

Tool: Gemini or ChatGPT image generation. Generate one room, judge it against the checklist below, then generate the others in the same chat and add "Same art style, camera height and lighting quality as the previous image" so the five match.

### How the still is used (read before prompting)

- The page covers the image with `object-fit: cover` and a slight blur, then puts the game panel on top. On a 1280 x 800 laptop the panel spans almost the full width and grows down from near the top, so what shows is **thin strips at the left and right edges, the top edge, and the floor area below a short panel** (start screens, debriefs). Wider screens show more of the sides. On phones the panel fills the screen, so phones mainly get the colour and mood.
- So put the room's character and signature props at the **far left and far right**, make the **lower third** an attractive floor (rug, floorboards, a crate or two at the edges) rather than a blank board, and keep the middle calm: no faces or text there.
- Image models garble lettering. Ask for at most one or two short signs per room, and treat them as optional: an image with clean blank signs is better than one with misspelt text.

### Shared style block (paste first, every time)

> A stylised 3D game environment render of the inside of a small Victorian room on a Thames wharf in the 1880s, in the style of a cosy low-poly adventure game: chunky simplified shapes with softly bevelled edges, flat matte materials with subtle hand-painted texture, warm toon shading, soft ambient occlusion, gentle bloom on light sources and light depth of field. Camera: eye level of a seated person, about 1.2 m high, facing straight into the room (one-point perspective), 35 mm lens, the back wall parallel to the frame, ceiling beams just visible at the top. Composition for a game background: the middle of the frame is a calm back wall with no characters, no text and no busy objects, because a game window will cover it; characters and the main props sit at the far left and far right of the frame, the top edge shows beams or windows, and the lower third is a pleasant, detailed floor (boards, a rug, small props near the edges), never an empty board or blank panel. Any characters are friendly anthropomorphic capybaras standing upright in Victorian work clothes, rounded and chunky, the same scale as the props, calm poses, not looking at the camera. 16:9 landscape, 1920 x 1080. No real-world logos, brands or people, no modern objects, no watermark, no border, no UI, and no lettering except the signs named below.

### Checklist before saving

- The centre band is calm: nothing important where the game panel will sit.
- No garbled or misspelt text, no modern objects, characters are capybaras.
- It reads as the same wharf as the hub: warm, cosy low-poly, not photoreal, not pixel art.
- Export as WebP at 1600 x 900, quality about 80 (try to keep it under about 150 KB), saved over `client/public/interiors/<id>.webp`. Squoosh (squoosh.app, free, runs in the browser) does the resize and WebP export in one step.
- Open the game page on a laptop and once at phone width to check the panel does not hide the best part.
